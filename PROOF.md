# A reduced-rank obstruction for both explicit pairs in Question 5.4

## Result

Let the stabilizer groups be the two pairs printed in Theorem 5.3 of Ali Assem Mahmoud, *Minimal Counterexamples of the MacWilliams Extension Theorem for Stabilizer Codes*, arXiv:2607.26214v1:

\[
S_A=\langle ZZZZI,XXIIX,IIXXX\rangle,\qquad
S_B=\langle ZZIZX,XIZXI,IXXYI\rangle,
\]

and

\[
S_+=\langle XXIIXX,IIXXXX,ZZZZIX\rangle,\qquad
S_-=\langle XXZXII,ZZXYII,IZZZXX\rangle.
\]

Neither pair of codespaces is mapped to the other by a product of one-qubit unitaries composed with a qubit permutation. This answers the first part of Question 5.4 negatively for both displayed pairs. It does not settle the more general question in the second sentence of Question 5.4.

## Lemma

Let \(S\) be an \(n\)-qubit stabilizer group generated by commuting, independent Pauli operators, with \(-I\notin S\), and let \(P_S\) be the orthogonal projector onto its codespace. For a set \(T\) of qubits, define

\[
S_T=\{g\in S:\operatorname{supp}(g)\subseteq T\}.
\]

Thus \(S_T\) is the subgroup of elements acting trivially on \(T^c\). It is not the punctured image of \(S\) on \(T\).

Then

\[
R_S(T):=\operatorname{rank}\!\left(\operatorname{Tr}_{T^c}P_S\right)
=\frac{2^{|T|}}{|S_T|}.
\]

### Proof

The stabilizer projector is

\[
P_S=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{g\in S}g.
\]

The partial trace of a Pauli tensor is zero if it acts nontrivially on any traced-out qubit. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{T^c}P_S
=\frac{2^{n-|T|}}{|S|}\sum_{g\in S_T}g_T.
\]

The operator \(|S_T|^{-1}\sum_{g\in S_T}g_T\) is the projector onto the common positive eigenspace of \(S_T\) on \(|T|\) qubits. If \(|S_T|=2^r\), that projector has rank \(2^{|T|-r}\). The omitted scalar is nonzero, so the displayed partial trace has the same rank. This gives \(R_S(T)=2^{|T|}/|S_T|\). QED.

## Local-unitary invariance

Suppose a product unitary \(U=U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_n\), together with a qubit permutation \(\pi\), maps one codespace projector to another. Partial trace commutes with the product action on the retained qubits, so the corresponding reduced operators are related by unitary conjugation after relabeling \(T\) by \(\pi\). Consequently

\[
R_S(T)=R_{S'}(\pi(T))
\]

for every subset \(T\). Thus the subset-indexed reduced-rank profile is an invariant of product-unitary equivalence up to qubit permutation.

## The five-qubit pair

Phases do not affect supports. Enumerating the eight elements of each stabilizer group shows that the only weight-three supports are

\[
S_A:\quad \{1,2,5\},\ \{3,4,5\},
\]

and

\[
S_B:\quad \{1,3,4\},\ \{2,3,4\}.
\]

There are no stabilizers of weight one or two. Hence, among the ten three-qubit subsets, exactly those two listed subsets have \(|S_T|=2\) and reduced rank \(2^3/2=4\); every other triple has reduced rank 8.

For \(S_A\), the two exceptional triples intersect in one qubit. For \(S_B\), they intersect in two qubits. A permutation preserves intersection cardinality. Therefore no qubit permutation can identify the reduced-rank profiles, and no product unitary followed by a permutation can map the two codespaces.

## The six-qubit pair

For \(S_+\), the three weight-four stabilizers have distinct supports

\[
\{1,2,3,4\},\quad \{1,2,5,6\},\quad \{3,4,5,6\}.
\]

There are no lower-weight stabilizers. Each of those four-qubit subsets therefore contains a subgroup of size 2 and has reduced rank \(2^4/2=8\). The other 12 four-qubit subsets have rank 16. The rank histogram is

\[
\{8:3,\ 16:12\}.
\]

For \(S_-\), all three weight-four stabilizers have support \(\{1,2,3,4\}\). Together with the identity they form a subgroup of size 4. That subset has reduced rank \(2^4/4=4\), while the other 14 four-qubit subsets have rank 16. The histogram is

\[
\{4:1,\ 16:14\}.
\]

The histograms differ, so no qubit permutation can identify the profiles. The two six-qubit codespaces are not product-unitarily equivalent up to permutation.

## Scope

This closes the two concrete equivalence tests in the first sentence of Question 5.4. The paper states that a negative answer strengthens Theorem 5.3 to the codespace level, and the five-qubit pair was the minimal open instance. The broader existence question for weight-isometric stabilizer codes that are locally unitary equivalent but not locally Clifford equivalent remains open.

The rank formula itself is standard in stabilizer theory. The contribution here is its subset-profile application to the two pairs posed in Question 5.4.
